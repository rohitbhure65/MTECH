CASE 1

Car Insurance Company

A car insurance company tracks its customers, the cars they own, and accident records associated with those cars. The key challenge is that one accident can involve multiple cars, making the Car–Accident relationship many-to-many.

Entities & Attributes

stringaddress
CAR

stringmodel
intyear

E-R Diagram (Crow's Foot)

Relationships & Cardinality

RelationshipBetweenCardinalityParticipationAttribute
owns CUSTOMER → CAR 1 : N Customer: total (must own ≥1 car)
Car: total (every car has an owner)		 —

Accident: total (at least one car)		 damage_amount

System Workflow

1
Customer Registration

A new driver is registered with driver_id, name and address. This creates a row in the CUSTOMER table.

2
Car Registration

Each car is linked to a customer via driver_id (FK). A customer may register multiple cars; each car belongs to exactly one customer.

3
Accident Reporting

When an accident occurs, a new ACCIDENT row is created with a unique report_no, date and location.

4
Participation Logging

Each car involved in the accident gets a row in PARTICIPATED with its own damage_amount. Multiple cars can share one report_no.

5
Claim Processing

Insurance can JOIN CUSTOMER → CAR → PARTICIPATED → ACCIDENT to compute payouts per driver per incident.

Key Use Cases (Sample Queries)

  • UC-1: List all cars owned by a specific customer and their total damage across all accidents.
  • UC-2: Find all accidents that occurred at a specific location in a given year.
  • UC-3: Identify cars with zero accident records (partial participation check).
  • UC-4: Find customers whose cars were involved in more than two accidents.
  • UC-5: Compute total damage_amount per accident (sum across all participating cars).

Relational Schema

CUSTOMER(driver_id, name, address)
CAR(license_no, model, year, driver_id)
ACCIDENT(report_no, acc_date, location)
PARTICIPATED(license_no, report_no, damage_amount)   ← resolves M:N
Why damage_amount is in PARTICIPATED, not ACCIDENT: Each car in the same accident may sustain a different amount of damage. Storing it in ACCIDENT would mean one fixed value for all cars — incorrect. Storing it in CAR would mix it with the car's own data. The (car, accident) pair in PARTICIPATED is the correct granularity.
CASE 2

University Registrar's Office

The university system separates a Course (general subject) from a Course Offering (a specific semester-run). This distinction is the central modelling decision. Course offerings are weak entities — they depend on the course for identification.

E-R Diagram (Crow's Foot)

Weak Entity — Double border on COURSE_OFFERING: An offering cannot be uniquely identified by (year, semester, section_no) alone. The same section number may exist in two different courses. The full key is: course_no + year + semester + section_no. This is why course_no is both PK and FK in COURSE_OFFERING.

Relationships & Cardinality

RelationshipBetweenCardinalityParticipationNotes
has_prerequisite COURSE ↔ COURSE (recursive) M : N Partial on both sides A course may have zero prerequisites
is_offered_as COURSE → COURSE_OFFERING 1 : N Course: partial. Offering: total Identifying (double line); course may not be offered yet
teaches INSTRUCTOR ↔ OFFERING M : N Offering: total. Instructor: partial "instructor(s)" in problem → multiple instructors per offering
enrols STUDENT ↔ OFFERING M : N Both: partial Resolved via ENROLMENT; grade can be NULL until awarded

System Workflow

1
Course Catalogue Creation

Admin adds courses with number, title, credits, syllabus. Prerequisites are linked via PREREQUISITE table.

2
Semester Planning

Before each semester, offerings are created (year, semester, section) and assigned instructors via TEACHES.

3
Student Registration

Students are registered in the system with student_id, name, program.

4
Enrolment

A student selects a specific offering. A row is inserted into ENROLMENT linking student_id to the full offering key. grade is NULL initially.

5
Grade Entry

After evaluation, instructor updates ENROLMENT.grade for each student in their offering.

6
Transcript Generation

Registrar JOINs ENROLMENT → COURSE_OFFERING → COURSE to produce student transcripts with course titles, credits, and grades.

Key Use Cases

  • UC-1: List all offerings of a given course across all semesters with instructor names.
  • UC-2: Find students enrolled in a specific section and their grades.
  • UC-3: Check whether a student has completed prerequisites before allowing enrolment.
  • UC-4: Compute CGPA by joining ENROLMENT with COURSE for credit-weighted grades.
  • UC-5: List all courses taught by a specific instructor across years.
  • UC-6: Find which courses have never been offered (outer join between COURSE and COURSE_OFFERING).

Relational Schema

COURSE(course_no, title, credits, syllabus)
PREREQUISITE(course_no, prereq_course_no)
COURSE_OFFERING(course_no, year, semester, section_no, timings, classroom)
TEACHES(instructor_id, course_no, year, semester, section_no)
STUDENT(student_id, name, program)
INSTRUCTOR(instructor_id, name, department, title)
ENROLMENT(student_id, course_no, year, semester, section_no, grade)
CASE 3

SCS Cricket League (SCL)

A cricket league database with teams, players, games and injury records. Key design decisions: captain as a recursive player reference (not a separate entity), two separate FK roles for games (host vs guest), and INJURY_RECORD as a weak entity dependent on PLAYER.

Entities & Attributes

stringteam_nameFK

stringgame_idPK
dategame_date
stringscore
stringhost_teamFK
stringguest_teamFK
INJURY_RECORD (weak)
stringplayer_idPK,FK
dateinjury_datePK
stringdescription

E-R Diagram (Crow's Foot)

Relationships & Cardinality

RelationshipBetweenCardinalityParticipation
hasTEAM → PLAYER1 : NBoth total — player must belong to a team; team must have players
captained_byTEAM ↔ PLAYER1 : 1Team: total (must have one captain). Player: partial (only some players are captains)
suffersPLAYER → INJURY_RECORD1 : NPlayer: partial (may have no injuries). Injury: total. Identifying relationship
host_teamTEAM → GAME1 : NGame: total (must have host). Team: partial
guest_teamTEAM → GAME1 : NGame: total (must have guest). Team: partial

System Workflow

1
Team Registration

League admin registers a team with name and city. Coach is stored as a string attribute (not a separate entity).

2
Player Registration

Players are added with position, skill level, and linked to exactly one team via team_name (FK).

3
Captain Assignment

A player from the team is designated captain. TEAM.captain_id is updated to that player's player_id. Integrity constraint: captain must belong to same team.

4
Game Scheduling

A game row is created with unique game_id, date, and two FK references to TEAM (host_team and guest_team). host ≠ guest must be enforced by a check constraint.

5
Score Recording

After the game, the score field is updated. Optionally score can be split into host_score and guest_score for richer querying.

6
Injury Tracking

When a player gets injured, a row is added to INJURY_RECORD with the player_id and injury_date as composite key. Cascades on player deletion.

Key Use Cases

  • UC-1: Find all players in a specific team along with their position and skill level.
  • UC-2: List all games played by a team (as either host or guest) in a given season.
  • UC-3: Identify players with more than two injury records in the current year.
  • UC-4: Find the captain of each team and their skill level.
  • UC-5: Find teams that have never played as a guest team (outer join check).
  • UC-6: List games where the same team was both host and guest — a constraint violation check (host_team ≠ guest_team).

Relational Schema

TEAM(team_name, city, coach, captain_id)
PLAYER(player_id, name, position, skill_level, team_name)
INJURY_RECORD(player_id, injury_date, description)
GAME(game_id, game_date, score, host_team, guest_team)
Extra constraints not visible in the ER diagram: (1) TEAM.captain_id must reference a PLAYER.player_id where that player's team_name = this team's team_name. (2) GAME.host_team ≠ GAME.guest_team. Both require CHECK constraints or triggers at the DB level.

Crow's Foot Notation — Quick Reference

Underlined attributes in Silberschatz ER notation: In traditional Chen/Silberschatz ER diagrams, primary key attributes are underlined and drawn in ovals. In crow's foot (as used in this assignment), they are shown as PK tags inside the entity table. Both convey the same information.
